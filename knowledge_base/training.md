# Training Theory

## Base Building
- **10% Rule**: Increase weekly volume gradually by no more than 10% to avoid overuse injuries and fatigue accumulation
- **Volume Progression**: Week 1 = 5 hours, add 30 minutes per week, keep at same level for 2 weeks before next increase
- **Recovery Ride Integration**: 1-2 easy Zone 1-2 rides per week between hard sessions debunks muscle fatigue
- **Zone 2 Focus**: 80% of base phase training at aerobic Zone 2 builds mitochondrial density and fat-oxidation capability
- **Consistency Principle**: Missing a hard workout is fine; skipped Zone 2 base rides accumulate as lost fitness
- **Progressive Overload**: Small consistent increases in duration/distance produce 3x better results than sporadic hard efforts
- **Low Heart Rate Training**: Training at low HR feels too easy but the magic happens in Zone 2 with time
- **Base Phase Duration**: 8-12 weeks recommended minimum, longer for athletes returning from injury or break

## Recovery Principles
- **Hard-Easy Alternation**: Never schedule two consecutive hard training days without an easy or rest day between them
- **48-Hour Recovery Window**: Muscles need minimum 48 hours between high-intensity sessions for glycogen replenishment and amino acid resynthesis
- **Sleep Quality**: 7-9 hours nightly is the most important recovery factor - more important than any supplement
- **Sleep Timing**: 10pm-6am sleep provides optimal circadian rhythm alignment for hormone production
- **Hydration Baseline**: Drink 500ml per hour of riding as minimum; increase by 25% when temperature exceeds 25C
- **Rest Days**: Minimum 1 full rest day per week - no structured rides, no training apps, just rest
- **Active Recovery**: 30-45 minute Zone 1 spinning promotes blood flow and accelerates clearance of metabolic waste
- **Recovery Nutrition**: Carbohydrate + protein ratio of 3:1 or 4:1 within 30-60 minutes post-ride maximizes glycogen refill

## Overtraining and Fatigue Management
- **Overtraining Signs**: Persistent fatigue (> 2 weeks), elevated resting heart rate (5+ BPM above baseline), exercise feels harder than usual, sleep disturbances, irritability
- **Overreaching vs Overtraining**: Functional overreaching (2-4 week block) leads to supercompensation; non-functional overreaching (>6 weeks) causes performance decline
- **Red Flags**: Declining performance on normalized watts, motivational burnout, frequent illness, irregular menstrual cycles in female athletes
- **If Overtrained**: Stop structured training for 1-2 weeks, focus on sleep 9+ hours, nutrition adequacy, gentle walking only
- **Monitoring Tools**: Use Resting HR, HRV, sleep quality, and perceived exertion to catch overtraining early
- **Rest vs Active Recovery**: Use complete rest for psychological fatigue; active recovery for physical fatigue

## Training Adaptations Timeline
- **Neural Adaptations**: First 2-4 weeks - improved coordination, motor unit recruitment, neural efficiency
- **Cardiovascular**: 4-8 weeks - increased plasma volume, improved cardiac output per beat
- **Mitochondrial**: 8-12 weeks - increased mitochondrial density, improved oxidative enzymes
- **Metabolic**: 12-16 weeks - enhanced fat oxidation at higher intensities, glycogen sparing
- **Structural**: 16+ weeks - capillary angiogenesis, muscle fiber type shifts, structural bone adaptations
- **Hormonal**: 6-8 weeks - improved testosterone:cortisol ratio, better anabolic environment

## Intensity Distribution Models
- **Polarized (80/20)**: 80% low intensity, 20% high - best for road cyclists and endurance specialists
- **Pyramidal (70/20/10)**: 70% low, 20% moderate, 10% high - common in amateur cycling
- **Threshold-Heavy**: More time at lactate threshold (Z3-Z4) - useful for time trialists and triathletes
- **Choose By Goal**: Criterium racers need more Z4-Z5; Gran Fondo riders need more Z2 base and Z3 threshold
- **Seasonal Shift**: More polarized in winter base phase, more threshold/VO2max in spring build, race-specific in summer

## Strength Training for Cyclists
- **Offseason Priority**: 2-3 strength sessions per week during base phase (November-February)
- **Exercises**: Squats, deadlifts, lunges (lower body); plank variations, dead bugs, Pallof press (core)
- **Periodization**: Hypertrophy phase (8-12 reps) in early base, strength phase (4-6 reps) mid-base, power phase (speed focus) pre-season
- **Core Work**: 15-20 minutes daily of targeted core work reduces back pain and improves pedaling stability
- **Upper Body**: Light upper body work maintains posture on long rides; rows, push-ups, overhead press
- **In-Season Maintenance**: 1-2 strength sessions per week during race season to maintain gains
- **Mobility**: Hip flexor stretches, thoracic spine mobility, ankle mobility - 10 minutes daily prevents injury

## Mental Training and Resilience
- **Pain Tolerance**: Mental toughness is trainable - push through discomfort in intervals to build threshold
- **Goal Breakdown**: Break big goals into quarterly, monthly, weekly, and daily process goals
- **Visualization**: 10-minute pre-ride visualization of successful execution improves actual performance
- **Self-Talk**: Replace negative thoughts with process-focused cues ("smooth pedaling", "relaxed shoulders")
- **Dealing with Bonking**: Mental preparation for low-energy moments - have a plan, have snacks accessible
- **Race-Day Mindset**: Focus on execution rather than outcome - controlling what you can control
- **Post-Crash Recovery**: Mental reset after crashes, mechanical issues, or bad races - 24-hour rule, move forward
