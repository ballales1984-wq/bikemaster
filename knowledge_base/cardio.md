# Cardiac Training

## Heart Rate Zones

### Zone Definitions (Five-Zone Model)
- **Zone 1** (50-60% max HR): Recovery rides, active rest days, warm-up/cool-down
- **Zone 2** (60-70% max HR): Aerobic base building, fat oxidation peak, long slow distance
- **Zone 3** (70-80% max HR): Tempo rides, lactate threshold development, sustainable intensity
- **Zone 4** (80-90% max HR): VO2 max intervals, 20-30 minute sustained efforts, threshold training
- **Zone 5** (90-100% max HR): Anaerobic sprints, attacks, max effort bursts, 30 seconds to 3 minutes

### Zone 2 Training Importance
Zone 2 is the most important zone for endurance athletes. 80% of training volume should be in Zone 2 for polarized training. Benefits include improved mitochondrial density, increased capillary development, enhanced fat oxidation, and a stronger aerobic base without accumulating fatigue.

### Lactate Threshold
- **Definition**: The exercise intensity where lactate begins to accumulate in the blood faster than it can be cleared
- **Typical Zone**: HR around 80-85% max HR for recreational riders, 85-90% for trained athletes
- **Improvement**: Can be raised by 5-15% through structured Zone 3-4 training over 12-16 weeks
- **Testing**: 30-minute time trial effort, average HR of last 20 minutes approximates lactate threshold HR

### Maximum Heart Rate
- **Formula 220-Age**: Rough estimate, +/- 12 BPM variation normal
- **LTHR Method**: More accurate: lactate threshold HR measured in field test
- **Sport-specific**: Cycling max HR is typically 5-10 BPM lower than running max HR
- **Age Decline**: Max HR declines ~0.75 BPM per year regardless of training level

### Heart Rate Variability (HRV)
- **Baseline**: Average HRV over 7 days; variations of 5-10 points normal day-to-day
- **Low HRV**: Indicates fatigue, stress, illness, or poor recovery - reduce training intensity
- **High HRV**: Indicates good recovery, low stress - safe to push harder
- **Monitoring**: Measure first thing in morning, before getting out of bed, for consistency
- **Training Implications**: Do hard efforts when HRV is above personal baseline; avoid when below

### Cardiac Drift
- **Definition**: Heart rate rising while power output stays constant or drops during prolonged exercise
- **Causes**: Dehydration, increased core temperature, reduced blood volume
- **Mitigation**: Hydration strategy, cooling strategies, electrolyte intake, heat acclimation
- **Detection**: Monitor HR:power ratio during long rides - a 5-10 BPM drift is common in 3+ hour rides

### Cardiovascular Adaptations to Training
- **Stroke Volume**: Increases 20-30% with endurance training, allowing more blood per beat
- **Resting HR**: Decreases from 60-80 BPM to 40-60 BPM in trained athletes (athlete's bradycardia)
- **VO2 Max**: Can improve 15-25% in beginners, 5-10% in experienced athletes over 6 months
- **Cardiac Output**: Max cardiac output increases from ~20 L/min to 30-35 L/min in elite endurance athletes
- **Capillary Density**: Increases 30-50% with consistent training, improving oxygen delivery to muscles

### Heart Rate Monitoring Best Practices
- **Sensor Placement**: Chest strap HR monitors are most accurate; optical wrist sensors have 5-10 BPM error
- **Warm-up Readiness**: HR responds within 30 seconds when changing intensity - more responsive than power
- **Pairing**: Upload HR data to training platform after each ride for long-term trend analysis
- **Lactate Testing**: If available, correlate HR zones with lactate measurements for personalized zones
