# Nutrition for Cyclists

## Daily Needs
- **Carbohydrates**: 5-7g per kg body weight for training days, 8-10g per kg for race/competition days
- **Proteins**: 1.4-1.8g per kg body weight daily for muscle recovery and repair
- **Fats**: 0.8-1.0g per kg body weight as primary energy source for endurance efforts
- **Water**: 500-750ml per hour on hot rides, 300-500ml on cooler rides
- **Caloric Baselines**: Sedentary BMR ~25 kcal/kg; add 400-600 kcal per hour of riding
- **Macro Split by Intensity**: High volume Zone 2 = higher fat proportion, high intensity = 60-70% carbs

## Carbohydrate Strategy
- **Pre-Ride (2-3 hours before)**: 100-150g carbs - oatmeal, banana, toast with jam, rice
- **During Ride**: 30-60g carbs per hour for rides over 1.5 hours, up to 90g for 3+ hour efforts
- **Carb Sources During Ride**: Energy gels (20-25g carbs each), chews, bananas, sports drinks
- **Post-Ride Window**: 1-1.2g carbs per kg body weight within 30-60 minutes after finishing
- **Glycogen Depletion**: After 90+ minutes of Zone 3-4 riding, glycogen stores are roughly 50% depleted
- **Consistent Supply**: Take small amounts every 15-20 minutes rather than large boluses
- **Sugar Types**: Mix glucose and fructose (2:1 ratio) for maximum absorption - up to 90g/hour
- **Avoid High-Fiber**: During rides, fiber causes GI distress - save high-fiber foods for off the bike

## Protein for Cyclists
- **Daily Target**: 1.6-2.2g per kg body weight for cyclists in training
- **Post-Ride Window**: 20-40g of protein within 30-60 minutes after rides over 90 minutes
- **Leucine Threshold**: 2-3g of leucine needed to trigger muscle protein synthesis per meal
- **Complete Proteins**: Essential amino acids from animal sources (whey, eggs, meat, fish) or plant combinations
- **Plant-Based Options**: Rice + pea protein, hummus + pita, quinoa, lentils, tofu
- **Before Bed**: 20-40g casein protein before sleep maximizes overnight muscle repair
- **Anti-Catabolic**: Protein intake prevents muscle breakdown during calorie deficits or long endurance efforts

## Hydration Strategy
- **Pre-Hydration**: 500-750ml water 2-3 hours before ride, another 250ml 15 minutes before start
- **During Ride**: 150-250ml every 15-20 minutes, adjust for temperature and sweat rate
- **Sweat Rate Test**: Weigh before and after 1 hour ride - 1kg lost = 1L fluid needed
- **Electrolytes**: Sodium 300-600mg per hour for rides over 1 hour, especially in heat
- **Overhydration Risk**: Hyponatremia from drinking too much without electrolytes - rare but dangerous
- **Morning Training**: Wake up 90 minutes early to drink 500ml water before morning ride
- **Urine Color Check**: Pale yellow = adequately hydrated, dark yellow = drink more

## On-Bike Nutrition
- **Rides Under 1 Hour**: Water only, no nutrition needed for healthy riders
- **Rides 1-2 Hours**: 30g carbs per hour (1 gel or 500ml sports drink)
- **Rides 2-3 Hours**: 45-60g carbs per hour (gels + bars + drink)
- **Rides 3+ Hours**: 60-90g carbs per hour, practice gut training to tolerate this volume
- **Energy Gel Protocol**: 1 gel every 30-45 minutes with water chase (not just gel)
- **Real Food Options**: Bananas, energy bars, rice cakes, jam sandwiches, dates
- **Preventing GI Distress**: Train your gut during training rides, not on race day
- **Temperature Effects**: In heat above 25C, increase fluid by 20%, decrease carb slightly

## Post-Ride Recovery Nutrition
- **30-60 Minute Window**: Optimal time for nutrient absorption - cellular doors are open
- **Carb-to-Protein Ratio**: 3:1 to 4:1 ratio of carbs to protein for glycogen refill + muscle repair
- **Example Meal**: 60g carbs + 15-20g protein - recovery shake, chocolate milk, or rice + chicken
- **Rehydration**: Drink 150% of weight lost in the 4 hours post-ride
- **Inflammation Reduction**: Tart cherry juice, omega-3 rich foods (salmon, walnuts) reduce muscle inflammation
- **Antioxidants**: Colorful vegetables (berries, spinach, peppers) combat exercise-induced oxidative stress
- **Electrolyte Replenishment**: Sodium, potassium, magnesium from coconut water or electrolyte tablets

## Weight Management
- **Fat Loss Strategy**: Moderate calorie deficit of 300-500 kcal per day for sustainable weight loss
- **Don't Cut Carbs Pre-Long-Ride**: Low carb intake before long rides leads to bonking and poor performance
- **Periodize Nutrition**: Build phase with surplus for muscle, transition phase with deficit for fat loss
- **Protein Preservation**: High protein intake (1.8-2.2g/kg) during deficit preserves muscle mass
- **Carb Timing**: Front-load carbs around training sessions, minimize carbs on rest days
- **Sustainable Rate**: 0.25-0.5kg per week fat loss is the healthy target
- **Ride Fueled**: Never train on empty stomach for sessions over 90 minutes

## Supplements (Evidence-Based)
- **Caffeine**: 3-6mg per kg body weight 45 minutes before ride - proven ergogenic aid
- **Creatine**: 3-5g daily - supports sprint power, no water retention issues for cyclists
- **Beta-Alanine**: 3-5g daily for 4+ weeks - buffers acid, helps with high-intensity efforts
- **Iron**: Critical for female cyclists and vegetarians - monitor ferritin levels every 6 months
- **Vitamin D**: Essential in winter months - 2000-4000 IU daily if levels are low
- **Omega-3**: 1-2g EPA+DHA daily for joint health and inflammation reduction
- **Nitrate (Beetroot Juice)**: 300-500mg nitrate 2-3 hours before - proven to reduce oxygen cost of exercise

## Common Nutrition Mistakes
- **Skipping Breakfast**: Impairs fat oxidation and reduces training quality
- **Under-Eating on Ride**: 30-60g carbs per hour never made anyone fat - bonking does
- **No Recovery Nutrition**: Post-ride nutrition is when you actually get faster
- **Relying on Supplements**: Real food should be primary, supplements fill gaps not replace meals
- **Ignoring Hydration**: Even 2% dehydration reduces performance by 10-15%
- **Trying New Foods Race Day**: Train your race-day nutrition strategy before the event
